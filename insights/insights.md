 Kwacha Bank | Banking Performance & Health

 Executive Summary

The Kwacha Bank dataset shows growth across several parts of the business, but the pace of growth differs considerably between customer acquisition, account openings and lending.

Between 2021 and 2025, the customer base increased by 21.0%, from 1,103 to 1,335 customers. Account openings increased much faster, rising 343.1%, from 847 to 3,753.

The deposit snapshot contains K2.031 billion in current balances. Deposit balances are concentrated across a small number of products, with the five largest account categories accounting for approximately 72.5% of balances.

Lending expanded sharply between 2023 and 2025. Annual disbursements increased from K54.1 million to K119.6 million, while the number of loans increased from 925 to 1,974.

The customer portfolio is predominantly low- and medium-risk, although 15.4% of customers are classified as high risk. The loan portfolio contains 76 records marked as defaulted, alongside written-off and overdue balances.

Fraud monitoring generated 43,483 alerts in the available 2025–2026 data. The majority were low-severity alerts, showing that the size of the monitoring workload is considerably larger than the number of alerts carrying the highest severity classifications.

Overall, the data points to a business where account activity and lending are expanding faster than the customer base. The next analytical step would be to establish whether that growth is translating into stronger customer value and risk-adjusted returns.

The customer base increased by 21.0% between 2021 and 2025, equivalent to an approximate annualised growth rate of 4.9% over the four-year interval.

The annual pattern is more informative than the overall percentage.

 2022: -1.3%
 2023: +10.1%
 2024: +7.8%

The bank therefore continued to add customers throughout the latter part of the period, but the rate of acquisition slowed in 2025.

This is different from saying that the customer franchise is contracting. The customer count is still moving upward; the change is in the rate of growth.

The current dataset contains 15,426 accounts for 10,000 customers, giving an average of approximately 1.54 accounts per customer.

This gap between customer growth and account growth is one of the more interesting findings in the dataset. It indicates that account creation is expanding much faster than the customer population.

The data alone does not explain the reason. It could reflect multiple accounts per customer, greater product penetration, changes in account-opening behaviour or other factors in the synthetic data.

 14. Data Quality Findings

The analysis uncovered several issues that are important to the interpretation of the results.

 Fraud total reconciliation

The correct fraud total is 43,483, not 53,483.

The annual totals and category breakdowns reconcile to the lower figure.

 Different time periods

Not all datasets support a 2021–2025 trend.

Customer acquisition and account openings have five-year coverage, loans have 2023–2025 coverage, while transactions and fraud have 2025–2026 coverage.

 Snapshot measures

Deposits and branch balances represent the current state contained in the dataset rather than a complete annual history.

 Loan denominator

The 5,000 loan records include rejected and approved records. Defaulted records should therefore not be presented as a formal default rate.

 Device data

The transaction device field covers only a subset of the one-million-transaction dataset.

 Branch proxy

The deposit-to-monthly-cost measure is a custom analytical measure rather than a standard banking efficiency ratio.

 15. What the Data Says About the Bank

The strongest pattern in the dataset is the difference in growth rates across business areas.

Customer acquisition increased moderately.

Account openings increased much faster.

Lending increased even faster between 2024 and 2025.

This suggests that the bank's activity is expanding, but the different components of that expansion need to be considered separately.

The deposit position is substantial in absolute terms within the synthetic dataset, but it is concentrated in a small number of products.

The loan portfolio is growing rapidly, which increases the importance of monitoring borrower quality, repayment behaviour and adverse loan statuses as the portfolio expands.

The risk data provides enough variation to support more advanced analysis, while the fraud data provides a useful starting point for evaluating alert volumes, investigation outcomes and detection methods.

 16. Further Analysis

The current project is primarily descriptive and diagnostic.

The next level would be to build models and metrics around the same banking data.

Potential extensions include:

 Credit Risk

Build a borrower-level probability-of-default model using:

 Credit score
 Income
 Employment
 Loan amount
 Loan category
 Existing exposure
 Repayment history
 Customer risk
 Default outcome

Model performance could then be evaluated using metrics such as ROC-AUC, precision, recall, confusion matrices and calibration.

 Fraud Detection

The fraud dataset could be developed into a classification problem using transaction behaviour, transaction value, velocity, channel, location and account history.

The investigation outcomes would provide the target variable for evaluating detection performance.

 Customer Profitability

Customer-level revenue and cost data could be added to estimate:

 Interest income
 Fees
 Deposit value
 Loan income
 Transaction activity
 Cost to serve
 Customer contribution

This would move the analysis from customer counts to customer economics.

 Product Penetration

The difference between 10,000 customers and 15,426 accounts creates an opportunity to analyse product holdings at customer level.

Measures could include:

 Products per customer
 Single-product customers
 Multi-product customers
 Product combinations
 Product adoption by segment
 Product adoption by province

---

 Conclusion

The Kwacha Bank analysis shows a business with meaningful expansion in customer activity, account openings and lending.

The most notable movement is not simply that the bank is growing, but that different parts of the business are growing at very different rates.

Customer acquisition increased by 21.0% between 2021 and 2025, while account openings increased by 343.1%. Lending disbursements then increased by 121.0% between 2023 and 2025.

At the same time, the data shows concentration in the deposit base, a measurable high-risk customer segment and a growing fraud-monitoring workload.

The current dataset is sufficient for descriptive banking performance analysis and provides a foundation for more advanced work in credit risk, fraud detection and customer profitability.

The main limitation is consistency of historical coverage. Extending the transaction, deposit and fraud datasets across the full 2021–2025 period would allow the next version of the analysis to connect growth, funding, lending, transaction activity and risk on the same timeline.
